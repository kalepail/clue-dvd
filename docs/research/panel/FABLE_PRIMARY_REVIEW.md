**Do not weaken this core.** The scheduler is fast (26 ms/mystery), reliable (500/500), and provably fair. Everything I propose below composes on top of it.

### 1.4 Where I correct or sharpen the brief

1. **The accusation-count "leak" is a false alarm — the missing penalty is the real mismatch.** (Incorporating the rulebook update; the earlier framing of the count as excess information is rejected.) The 0–4 count without its card-payment cost changes the *economics*, not the information: in the physical game each probe of the solution costs up to four item cards and can eliminate you, so accusations are terrifying; in the app they are free oracle queries that only cost a turn. That makes spam-accusing a dominant strategy in the app and is the single largest fair-play distortion in the current shell. See F-T2.
2. **The brief's turn-shell section under-weights one wiring gap:** the world simulator *already* generates room closures (`world-sim.ts:341-352`) and the host shell *already* has locked-room UI — they are simply never connected (`ai-mystery-setup.ts:64` hard-codes `lockedRooms: []`). Restoring rulebook locked doors is therefore mostly plumbing, not new simulation. See F-T5.
3. **A structural negative-space signal the brief misses:** the answer location is excluded from *all* non-gathering placement *all day* — social circles (`world-sim.ts:410`), texture solos (`:497-501` draw from `socialRooms`), and threads (`:649`) never enter it at any hour, and `room_undisturbed` facts exclude it by truth (`fact-harvest.ts:493-497`). Only scheduled gatherings and the late item-anchor/home facts can mention it. Over a case, the theft room is systematically less "lived-in" than every other room. This is both a subtle meta-tell for repeat players and an unnecessary variety loss, because occupancy at *non-theft* hours is perfectly safe (facts derived from it remain true and cannot kill the answer cell). See F-W4.
4. **Two internal doc/code mismatches the brief doesn't list:** the scheduler's own header comment states final windows "suspects 2–4, items 2–3, locations 2–3, times 2–3" (`clue-scheduler.ts:13`) which contradicts `FINAL_TARGET` twelve lines below (the living doc `AI_ENGINE.md:37` is correct); and `verifyClosing` uses a plain lowercase substring check (`clue-verifier.ts:111-115`), so an answer time of "Night" is satisfied by the word "Midnight" (or "tonight") appearing in the closing — the boundary-aware `CARD_MATCHERS` (`clue-verifier.ts:38-44`) are not used there. Low practical risk (the closing prompt spells out all four names) but a real verification hole. See F-P3.
5. **A planned fact vocabulary was never built:** `AI_ENGINE_V3_PLAN.md:82-99` specifies `key_event` ("the display-case key was missing from its hook by breakfast" — corpus mystery #1) and `cross_category` Inspector facts; neither exists in `FactKind` (`fact-harvest.ts:31-46`). These are precisely the mechanisms the original notes used to feel distinct. See F-S3.

### 1.5 New quantitative findings from my instrumentation

- **The converged pair is nearly constant:** item+time in 95.4% of cases. Combined with time reaching a *single* candidate in 57.8% of cases, most games have the same macro-shape: public evidence tells you *what* was taken and *when*; your dealt cards tell you *who* and *where*. Fair, but repetitive across replays — and it systematically devalues dealt **time** cards (usually redundant with public clues) while over-weighting suspect/location cards.
- **Final items count is bimodal — never 4 in 500 cases** (2: 177, 3: 302, 5: 21). This is the decoy mechanism's fingerprint (`world-sim.ts:316-324`): the item axis either closes onto answer+decoys (2–3) or fails to converge (5). A repeat player can learn: "the 2–3 valuables nobody vouched for late in the day include the stolen one, and the theft hour is just after the big 'all still where they belonged' clue" (the always-generated anchor bundle, `world-sim.ts:606-613`, `fact-harvest.ts:374-402`). Each single case is fair; the *meta-pattern* telegraphs.
- **`solo_presence` was scheduled once in 6,000 reveals**, so the "thief briefly unaccounted for, only in the last two clues" drama rule (`clue-scheduler.ts:637-638`, `:658`) is effectively dead code in practice — the greedy never finds solo facts competitive.

### 1.6 Late brief updates — independently verified

Three further evidence points were added to the brief during this review; I checked each against the code before incorporating them:

1. **Case budget is a project decision, not an authenticity requirement.** All ten transcribed originals have **exactly 8 Butler testimonies**, while Inspector notes vary **1–6** per case (verified by counting `data/mysteries.json`: clue counts 8×10; note counts 3,1,3,1,3,4,6,3,3,5). V3's fixed 10 clues + exactly 2 notes (`REVEAL_COUNT`, `NOTE1_POSITION`, `NOTE2_POSITION` at `clue-scheduler.ts:74-76`; `EXPERT_CLUE_COUNT` at `ai-mystery-setup.ts:7`) is therefore a design choice. See F-S5.
2. **The verifier checks names, not meaning.** `verifyClueText` flags only unlicensed card names and empty text, with sentence-count *warnings* (`clue-verifier.ts:78-100`). Nothing checks semantic entailment, exact scope ("everyone" vs. the listed subset), polarity, chronology, or invented non-card people. The file's own header claims invented people "are caught by the cast prompt + audit warnings" (`clue-verifier.ts:11-12`) — but `NON_CARD_NAME_WHITELIST` is exported (`ai-v3-prompts.ts:213-223`) and **never imported anywhere**; no such audit exists. The truth boundary between fact and player currently rests on prompt compliance alone. See F-P5.
3. **The pre-generated closing is path-dependent on evidence nobody may have seen.** `buildClosingPrompt` receives `caseRecap: storySeeds.map(seed => seed.brief)` — all twelve briefs including both private notes — labeled "The evidence that was revealed during play" (`ai-mystery-engine.ts:259-265`, `ai-v3-prompts.ts:195-196`), plus `finalCandidates`, the field *after both notes* (`clue-scheduler.ts:420-426`). The closing is generated at scenario creation, before any turn is played, so it cannot know whether anyone read either note — and my reproduction of the public-only sweep (§1.1) shows the ten public facts alone leave a far broader field (means 7.83/4.16/7.75/3.72) than the with-notes finals the closing narrates. See F-P4.

## 2. Findings catalog

Labels: **[VD]** Verified defect or mismatch · **[SC]** Supported design concern · **[OE]** Optional experiment · **[RN]** Rejected/non-issue. Each entry: confidence, evidence, counterargument, impact.

### World layer

**F-W1 [SC] Occasion families are prose paint, not world structure.** Confidence: high. Evidence: §1.3 row 1. Fourteen families (`ai-mystery-engine.ts:57-72`) produce identical evidence topologies; a recital and a horticultural exhibition can share the same day. Contrast the corpus: in "A Bad Sport" the games *are* the alibi structure; in "The Last Straw" the fountain meeting schedule *is* the time chain (`original-mysteries.ts:57-73`, `:192-210`). Counterargument: the dossier + few-shot rotation already yields distinct-sounding prose, and players see one case at a time. Response: the user's complaint is about *repeated* games; prose variety without evidence-topology variety exhausts quickly. Impact: high on replayability. Falsifier: if a blind reader of 5 fact-schedules (no prose) can't cluster them by family today *and* players report cases feel distinct anyway, this concern is overweighted.

**F-W2 [SC] Catalog narrowness bounds everything downstream.** Confidence: high. Evidence: `world-sim.ts:114-195` (§1.3 row 2). The renderer cannot invent events (mention licenses forbid it), so total event variety per case ≈ these catalogs. Counterargument: the corpus itself proves coarse events suffice (`AI_ENGINE_V3_PLAN.md:183`), and catalogs are cheap to extend. Response: agreed — this is exactly why the fix is data-shaped, not architectural. Impact: medium-high, cumulative with F-W1.

**F-W3 [SC] Items are passive inventory, not actors.** Confidence: high. Evidence: `ItemState` (`world-sim.ts:49-56`) holds only home/displayed/offsite/sightings; the only lifecycle events are one optional offsite item (`:619-627`) and secured sets (`:327-338`). The corpus shows items being borrowed for costumes, polished during dinner, used to keep score, lent and left on chairs (`original-mysteries.ts:42-54`, `:61-66`, `:158`). The `borrowed_item` thread exists but is mention-only color (`fact-harvest.ts:639-640`). Impact: high — 33.9% of all reveals are "item X was still in its place," the single biggest genericity driver. Counterargument: item facts are the load-bearing time-killers; making them exotic risks the late-theft closure basket (`clue-scheduler.ts:541-561`). Response: add *semantically equivalent but narratively varied* item facts (polishing bundle = `item_intact` with a different brief; handled-during-game = `item_intact` with witness color) rather than new kill semantics first.

**F-W4 [VD] Answer-room negative space (over-exclusion).** Confidence: high (behavior verified; player-perceptibility is medium). Evidence: §1.4.3. The all-day exclusion is stronger than the invariant requires ("thief alone at answer location at answer time" needs exclusion *only at the answer time* — `world-sim.ts:517-519` already implements the per-slot rule for circles, making the global `socialRooms` filter at `:410` redundant belt-and-suspenders that leaks pattern). Safe fix: allow non-thief occupancy of the answer room at non-answer slots; harvested `group_presence`/`item_intact` facts from it remain true (intact sightings of the answer item before the theft are already legal, `:595`). Impact: medium — removes a meta-tell, adds honest room variety. Falsifier: instrument "answer-room positive mentions per case" before/after; if players in blind tests never exploit negative space, downgrade to [SC].

**F-W5 [RN] Movement adjacency as a gameplay defect.** Confidence: high. It is a **[VD] documentation mismatch** (`AI_ENGINE_V3_PLAN.md:72` vs `world-sim.ts:399-564`) but **not** a gameplay defect: time slots are hour-plus intervals (Dawn→Breakfast…), so any room is reachable between slots; briefs never narrate transit, so no contradiction can surface in prose. Fix the doc line; adjacency simulation would add cost and constraint for zero player-visible truth gain. Optional: a soft preference for adjacent drift would marginally improve "realism" of multi-slot group merges — not worth the complexity now.

### Fact layer

**F-F1 [SC] Kill-semantics vocabulary is item/room-check heavy and two planned kinds are missing.** Confidence: high. Evidence: 15 kinds exist (`fact-harvest.ts:31-46`); `key_event` and `cross_category` from the plan (`AI_ENGINE_V3_PLAN.md:92-97`) were never built; 52.5% of scheduled reveals are `item_intact`+`room_undisturbed`. Impact: high — this, not the renderer, is the root of "generic notes" (see F-S2). Counterargument: every kind that exists is corpus-attested and safe; missing kinds are additive work. Response: yes — that's the point; the fix is supply-side.

**F-F2 [VD] Thread briefs spoil their own mystery.** Confidence: high. Evidence: `fact-harvest.ts:638-644` — quarrel/errand briefs embed "as it turned out"/"In truth they were…" so the *single* scheduled reveal both raises and resolves suspicion. Setup/payoff staging is impossible with one fused brief. Impact: medium-high on round-to-round tension. This is a concrete, isolated defect in the brief-authoring layer, not the architecture.

**F-F3 [VD, minor] Answer-dependent asymmetry in solo briefs.** Confidence: high (existence), low (impact). Evidence: `fact-harvest.ts:256` — an innocent solo brief includes the activity; the answer suspect's solo brief silently omits it. The answer-blind renderer thus receives a *distinguishable* brief shape for the thief's solo. Mitigated by the position ≥11 rule and by the fact it was scheduled once in 6,000 reveals. Fix for principle: give the thief's solo a neutral honest activity phrase ("was not seen by anyone who can say").

### Scheduler layer

**F-S1 [SC] Coverage-first greedy with no experiential objective.** Confidence: high. Evidence: selection scores only `axisDeaths*120 + pairProgress*10` (`clue-scheduler.ts:523`); ordering only enforces checkpoints + gentle-early/heavy-late (`:669-687`); `softPenalty` (axis repetition + suspect-mention spread, `:730-758`) is computed once and returned, never optimized (§1.3 row 5). There are no constraints on fact-kind diversity, note identity, thread staging, occasion relevance, or which axes converge. Impact: high — this is the *cheapest high-leverage fix in the codebase*, because attempts cost microseconds and all hard invariants are checked per-candidate: generate K schedules, pick the best by an extended score. Counterargument: any new soft objective risks starving feasibility. Response: best-of-K sidesteps that entirely — every candidate already passed the hard gates; you only ever choose among proven-fair schedules.

**F-S2 [SC→VD-adjacent] Note selection has no identity.** Confidence: high. Evidence: notes are just "any `noteSuitable` fact that lands at positions 6/9" (`clue-scheduler.ts:649-663`, `needsNoteSlot :718-724`); `noteSuitable` is true for nearly every constraining kind. Result (measured): 69.9% of notes are room-check/item-accounting; nothing prevents N1 and N2 being the *same kind*, or a note duplicating an adjacent clue's semantics. The originals' notes are cross-category, meta, or schedule facts ("The theft did not occur in a Location that a game was played at", "room name was only one word", per-suspect meeting times — `original-mysteries.ts:68-71`, `:143-149`, `:203-208`). Impact: high on the note experience specifically called out by the user (`TOPROMPT.md:20`).

**F-S3 [SC] Converged-axis monoculture (item+time 95.4%).** Confidence: high (measurement), medium (how much players notice). Evidence: §1.5; cause is structural — decoys make items *always* tractable, and the late-theft basket plus discovery facts make time tractable, so `convergeOrder`'s sort-by-count (`clue-scheduler.ts:573-576`) almost always picks them. `AI_ENGINE.md:37` claims "which two varies by seed — that variety is part of replayability"; at 95.4/4.2/0.4 this claim is **not currently true** — a third doc/behavior mismatch. Impact: high on repeat play; also drives the dead-time-card problem (57.8% of games end with times=1). Dependency: steering toward location or suspect-side convergence needs more location-cutting fact supply (F-F1) — the world currently generates at most one closure and undisturbed bundles.

**F-S4 [OE] Dramatic-shape targets (surprise, reinterpretation, per-case arc).** Confidence: medium. The brief lists "reveal-level surprise or reinterpretation" as missing. True, but I rank it below diversity/notes/threads: surprise emerges naturally once threads stage properly and mechanisms vary. Treat as a scoring term in best-of-K (e.g., reward a late fact that re-frames an early color fact), not as a hard system.

**F-S5 [OE] Fixed 10+2 case budget vs. the corpus's 8 + 1–6.** Confidence: high (fact), medium (whether to act). Evidence: §1.6.1. The originals prove the format tolerates variable note counts and a smaller clue budget; V3's 10+2 is a project decision entangled with the checkpoint positions (6 and 9) and the shell's note-unlock gates (`useGameStore.ts:527-531`). Variable budgets (e.g., 10 clues + 2–4 notes, extra notes being lighter "lens" facts) would directly serve note variety (F-S2) and private-information play (Q8) — more distinct private edges to spend turns on — at the cost of generalizing the checkpoint machinery and the unlock gating. Counterargument: two well-differentiated notes may deliver most of the value at a fraction of the complexity; notes also carry heavy deduction weight (§1.6.3), so *more* notes deepens the optional-private-evidence tension unless F-P4's fix lands first. Recommendation: defer until after Phase 4; run as an experiment gated on the Phase 0 telemetry. Dependency: F-P4 (closing honesty) and a public-only convergence floor (Q8) must land first.

### Prose layer

**F-P1 [RN] "The renderer is the source of genericity."** Rejected as a root cause. The renderer faithfully dresses what it is given (`ai-v3-prompts.ts:100-137`); with 52.5% of inputs being accounting facts, no prompt work can fix the output mix. Prompt-only fixes are the one intervention the evidence rules out. (The brief's caution §"Important cautions" agrees; I confirm it from the mention-license design — the renderer *cannot* add events.)

**F-P2 [SC, minor] Few-shot rotation is narrow.** `pickFewshots` draws 3 mysteries, ~9 clues (`ai-v3-prompts.ts:40-54`) from a 10-mystery corpus; fine, but the corpus's *structural* variety (meeting schedules, costume notes, letter-Y note) is exactly what the fact layer can't express yet, so the few-shots over-promise relative to the seeds. Resolves itself with F-F1.

**F-P3 [VD, minor] `verifyClosing` substring hole.** §1.4.4. Fix: reuse the boundary matchers. One-line change, add a Night/Midnight regression test.

**F-P4 [VD] The closing narrates a path nobody may have walked.** Confidence: high. Evidence: §1.6.3 — the closing prompt presents all twelve briefs (both private notes included) as "evidence that was revealed during play" and narrates the with-notes final field, yet it is generated before play and notes are optional, private, turn-costed actions. Concretely: a table where no one read N2 can hear a closing citing N2's fact as shared evidence, and "the field still standing" it describes (e.g., times down to one) can be far narrower than what the table's public evidence actually left (public-only means 7.83/4.16/7.75/3.72 — §1.1). This violates the project's own truthfulness bar: every player-facing claim should be true *of the game the table actually played*. Counterargument: the closing hedges with "the detectives' own dealt cards settled those final distinctions" (`ai-v3-prompts.ts:197-204`), and most tables likely do read both notes since they're strong. Response: "likely" is not a guarantee, and the phrasing "revealed during play" is unconditionally wrong for note facts at generation time. Fix (choose one, in order of preference): (a) **path-independent closing** — build `caseRecap` from the ten Butler briefs only, pass the *public-only* field as "what the testimony left standing," and add one honest conditional sentence for the file: "the Inspector's file held two further notes for those who consulted it"; (b) pass both fields (public-only and with-notes) with phrasing that attributes note facts to the file, never to shared play. Either is a small, contained change to `buildClosingPrompt` inputs (`ai-mystery-engine.ts:259-265`) — the public-only field is computable from the existing kill lists (I did it in 20 lines; §1.1). Impact: medium-high on trust — the closing is the case's final impression and currently the only piece of player-facing prose that can misstate what happened at the table. Acceptance: a verifier rule that the closing names no note-only fact without file attribution; regression test with a schedule whose N2 is load-bearing.

**F-P5 [VD → SC] The truth boundary is prompt-deep: no entailment, scope, polarity, chronology, or invented-person verification.** Confidence: high (gap's existence), medium (in-practice failure rate — unmeasured). Evidence: §1.6.2; `verifyClueText` (`clue-verifier.ts:78-100`) enforces the mention license and emptiness only; the render prompt's "do not drop, soften, or extend the stated scope" (`ai-v3-prompts.ts:121`) is unverified; the header's claimed invented-people audit does not exist (`NON_CARD_NAME_WHITELIST` is dead code, exported at `ai-v3-prompts.ts:213` and imported nowhere). Failure modes that would silently break fair play: a testimony rendering "most of the guests" as "everyone" (players over-eliminate), inverting polarity ("was seen" vs "was not seen"), shifting an hour, or inventing "the chauffeur" as a witness. The scheduler's proof covers *which facts* are revealed, not *what the prose actually asserts*. Mitigation — and this is the strongest single recommendation of this addendum: **adopt the DVD-authentic evidence capsule.** The original disc displayed an on-screen write-down summary while Ashe spoke; the pipeline already possesses a deterministic, truthful, per-reveal summary (the fact's `writerBrief`, or a tighter capsule derived from `mentions` + kind). Display the capsule as the canonical "write this down" line alongside (or after) Ashe's narration, host and phone. Then prose drift is *cosmetically* possible but *deductively* harmless — the truth channel is deterministic end-to-end, closing the only remaining LLM-dependent link in the fair-play chain. Cheaper and more robust than an entailment-checking model call; an entailment spot-check can still be added later as telemetry (not a gate). Counterargument: two texts per clue could clutter the reveal moment; mitigate with progressive disclosure (narration first, capsule persists in the log/notes view). Falsifier for the "SC" grading of in-practice risk: run `eval-mysteries.ts --ai` over ~30 seeds and hand-audit scope/polarity fidelity; if drift is ≥ a few percent, this is a hard [VD] in effect, not just in principle.

### Turn-shell layer (against the OCR-verified rulebook)

**F-T1 [VD] Summon-the-Butler ritual lost.** "Reveal Clue" advances the turn with no private item-card draw instruction and no Evidence-Room prohibition (`GamePage.tsx:1063-1070`, `useGameStore.ts:397-437`). The rulebook: a summon adds the top Butler's Pantry item card privately to the summoner's hand and is forbidden in the Evidence Room. Impact: high — this is the entire item-card economy's faucet; without the ritual, the physical Pantry sits unused and "Look at Item Card" has nothing to look at. Fix is a ritual/reminder + host log (never hand tracking — the app cannot see hands).

**F-T2 [VD] Accusation penalty is partially implemented (count itself is authentic — earlier leak framing is rejected).** Confidence: high. §1.3 (corrected row) and §1.4.1. The host result panel already instructs "Turn in {N} card(s) face up to the Evidence Room" (`AccusationPanel.tsx:261`), so the gap is narrower than first framed. ## 3. Answers to the required review questions

**Q1. Important / minor / false alarms.**
Genuinely important: F-S1 (no experiential objective), F-S2 (note identity), F-S3 (converged-axis monoculture + dead time cards), F-F1/F-W3 (fact/item supply), F-F2 (thread self-spoiling), F-P4 (path-dependent closing), F-P5 (prompt-deep truth boundary — fix via the DVD-authentic evidence capsule), F-T1 (butler ritual), F-T6 (event economy), F-W1 (occasion inertness).
Important but narrower than first framed: F-T2 (accusation penalty — host text exists; wrong card type, missing on phone, unrecorded, no elimination).
Minor: F-W4 (negative space), F-P3 (verifier substring), F-T3/T4/T7 (passage variant, note inconsistency, suggestion logging), F-F3, F-D1.
Optional experiments: F-S4 (dramatic shape scoring), F-S5 (variable case budget — the corpus's 8 clues + 1–6 notes proves 10+2 is a project choice).
False alarms: accusation count as a leak (authentic — rejected per rulebook); movement adjacency as gameplay defect (doc-only); renderer as *genericity* root cause (it remains a *truth-boundary* risk, F-P5 — the two are distinct); any suggestion the scheduler is unreliable or needs replacement.

**Q2. Root causes of genericity, by layer.**
- *World:* one generic day-generator; occasion unused (F-W1); ~40 texture strings (F-W2); items inert (F-W3); answer-room over-exclusion (F-W4).
- *Facts:* vocabulary skew — the only abundant, cheap, always-true facts are accounting facts; planned join/key kinds unbuilt (F-F1); threads emit one fused self-resolving brief (F-F2).
- *Schedule:* greedy optimizes coverage only; diversity/notes/threads/axes unpriced; first-feasible wins (F-S1–S3). The 52.5% accounting share is the *interaction* of fact supply (harvest emits ~20+ item/room facts per world) with a greedy that finds them efficient.
- *Prose:* not a root cause (F-P1); it faithfully amplifies upstream monotony.
- *Round:* nothing changes between reveals; interruptions are wall-clock and generic; no event economy (F-T6); reveal-and-pass turn shape (F-T1).
- *Physical/digital loop:* the app dropped the rituals that make physical components matter (pantry draw, accusation card payment, locked doors), so the physical layer stops feeding tension back into rounds (F-T1/T2/T5).

**Q3. A single case's dynamic arc (10 butler opportunities + 2 note windows).**
Target shape, expressed as scheduler-checkable properties, not vibes:
- *C1–C3 (establish):* occasion-anchored facts (arrival/gathering/first thread setup); ≥2 distinct kinds; no kind repeated; broad time kills only; ≥1 pure-color fact planting a thread (setup half only).
- *C4–C5 + N1 (complicate):* first item accounting *or* movement chain; thread escalation (second sighting, still unresolved); N1 = a "lens" fact of a kind unused so far, preferably cross-category/key-style once F-F1 lands.
- *C6–C7 + N2 (turn):* discovery or secured-set beat; the day's one structural event (closure/departure) if unused; N2 = "keystone" — the heaviest private cut, kind ≠ N1's kind and ∉ adjacent clues' kinds.
- *C8–C10 (converge):* anchor bundle; thread payoff (innocent truth revealed — currently fused into setup, F-F2); answer-adjacent mentions (already ≥7); heaviest kills last (already implemented).
Acceptance: per-case kind entropy ≥ threshold; no 3 consecutive same-kind; thread setup≤4 & payoff≥8 whenever a thread is scheduled; N1.kind ≠ N2.kind; all existing hard invariants unchanged. All of this is expressible as best-of-K scoring terms (F-S1) plus two hard ordering rules (thread spacing, note distinctness).

**Q4. Occasions altering deterministic truth safely.**
Make `occasionFamily` an *input* to `simulateWorld`'s catalog selection (it is already passed in — `world-sim.ts:208` — and already chosen before the world, `ai-mystery-engine.ts:151`): per-family gathering tables (recital → Ballroom rehearsals at T05/T07; horticultural → Conservatory/Rose Garden slots; tournament → repeated Billiard Room fixtures), family activity/thread/errand catalogs, family item-display bias (`collector's viewing` → several items `displayedForOccasion`, giving honest early item texture), and optionally one family "program event" (a meeting schedule à la corpus #10 → a chain of `group_presence`/`personal_remark` facts). Safety argument: the family only selects *which truth-generating catalog entries* are eligible; every invariant lives below that layer (gatherings still avoid the answer time, threads still exclude the thief, etc.), and `assertFactsSpareAnswer` plus the scheduler's checks are unchanged. Risk: a family could starve the scheduler (e.g., too few eligible gatherings) — mitigated because the engine already retries across worlds (`ai-mystery-engine.ts:170-184`) and eval will show per-family success rates. Acceptance: 200 seeds × 14 families → 100% schedule success; measurable divergence in fact-kind and location distributions across families; dossier references only events that exist in facts.

**Q5. Items dynamic without breaking the answer or card economy.**
Three safe moves, in increasing ambition: (a) *reskin the workhorses* — polishing/inventory/handled-during-play variants of `item_intact` (same kill semantics, richer witness briefs; corpus #8's polishing line is exactly this); (b) *new truthful kinds*: `item_moved` (kept in L1 until T, then displayed in L2 — kills (i, l∉{L1,L2}) with time refinement), `key_event` (case key missing by T → lower-bounds the theft hour; corpus #1), lend/return events for non-answer items (borrowed openly, returned — kills nothing or bounds like intact); (c) *answer-item pre-theft life* — the answer item may be truthfully seen, admired, even handled *before* the theft hour (sightings ≤ theft−1 are already legal, `world-sim.ts:595`), which lets it appear naturally in early color instead of only in the late anchor. Hard constraints preserved: never claim the answer item intact/secured/moved-away at or after the theft hour; decoys keep the item axis honestly open; the physical Pantry supply is untouched (these are narrative facts, not card effects). Acceptance: `item_intact` share of reveals drops below ~25% with no loss of schedule success; item-axis final windows unchanged.

**Q6. Thread arcs without misleading noise.**
Split each thread into stage-facts at harvest: `thread_setup` (mention-only; suspicious observation, cause withheld), `thread_echo` (optional second sighting), `thread_payoff` (either mention-only disclosure of the innocent cause, or — better — a *constraining* alibi that clears the thread suspect, since thread suspects are never the thief, `world-sim.ts:641`). Scheduler rules: schedule a payoff for every scheduled setup (hard); setup ≤ position 4, payoff ≥ position 8 (hard); at most 2 threads staged per case; extend `softPenalty`'s mention-count logic so thread suspects aren't over-mentioned relative to the cast (`clue-scheduler.ts:740-756` already does this for the answer suspect). Anti-noise guarantees: threads never assert false facts (already true — they are real world events); payoff always lands before the end, so no innocent player-character ends the game under manufactured unresolved suspicion; setup briefs must be observations ("kept disappearing toward the service side"), never accusations. My instrumentation makes the baseline falsifiable: today 40% of cases schedule zero thread facts and 0% stage a setup/payoff pair; target ≥80% of cases with ≥1 complete staged arc.

**Q7. Scheduler diversification preserving invariants.**
Priority order: (1) **Best-of-K selection** — run the existing attempt loop to collect K (5–10) *valid* schedules, score each with an extended experiential score (kind-diversity entropy, axis alternation, note-kind distinctness, thread staging, converged-pair novelty vs recent games, suspect-mention spread — the current `softPenalty` becomes one term), return the argmax. Zero risk to invariants (every candidate already passed all hard checks); cost ≈ K×26 ms. (2) **Hard micro-rules** where soft scoring is insufficient: N1.kind ≠ N2.kind; ≤2 `room_undisturbed` and ≤3 `item_intact` facts per schedule (feasibility-checked by eval before enforcement — if success rate drops below ~99% on 500 seeds, relax to scoring terms). (3) **Converged-pair steering**: seed-weighted target pair chosen up front (e.g., 55% item+time, 25% location+time, 20% item+location), used to reorder `convergeOrder` (`clue-scheduler.ts:573-576`) with fallback to tractability on failure — dependent on F-F1/F-W3 supply for the non-item pairs, so sequence it after Phase 2. Also revisit `FINAL_TARGET.times.min = 1` → consider 2 (times fully solved in 57.8% of games makes dealt time cards dead; the corpus does collapse times sometimes, so keep min 1 but *score against* it in best-of-K rather than forbid).

**Q8. Distinct jobs for Note 1 and Note 2; private info at the table.**
N1 (position 6, mid-game) = *lens*: reframes existing public evidence (cross-category/key/schedule fact) — rewards the player who connects it to clues already heard; low kill-weight, high interpretive value. N2 (position 9) = *keystone*: the heaviest private cut in the case; arriving after checkpoint B it can safely bite hard. Both: kind-distinct from each other and from adjacent clues (F-S2). Table dynamics: private notes should create *observable behavior changes* (suddenly confident suggestions), which is the fun; the app supports this by (a) never announcing which category a note touched, (b) keeping first-read-costs-a-turn (a real price for private edge — defensibly consistent with the rulebook's note action), (c) logging structured suggestions (F-T7) so the table can notice behavioral shifts. Do not convert notes to public — privacy is the only asymmetric-information mechanic the game has besides hands.

One structural tension the public-only measurement (§1.1) exposes: **the notes are load-bearing in the math but optional in the shell.** The designed final windows are only reached if both notes' facts apply; the ten public facts alone leave means of 7.83 suspects and 7.75 locations. That is *safe* (wider is never unfair) but it means a note-averse table gets a much flabbier endgame than the design intends, and it is the root of the closing-honesty defect (F-P4). Two complementary responses: (a) add a **public-only convergence floor** to the scheduler — a third checkpoint asserting the ten Butler facts alone land within a looser bounded window (current means suggest e.g. suspects ≤ 8, items ≤ 5, locations ≤ 8, times ≤ 4 is already nearly free) so the game degrades gracefully without notes; (b) make the closing path-independent (F-P4 fix). Together these make note-reading a genuine *edge*, not a hidden requirement.

**Q9. Turn-shell: restore / retain-as-variant / remove.**
Restore to rulebook (default rules): Summon-the-Butler ritual with pantry-draw instruction + Evidence-Room prohibition (F-T1); accusation penalty ritual with acknowledgement (F-T2); locked doors from `world.roomClosure` (F-T5); secret passages as movement/locked-state only (F-T3); note re-read free after first read on both host and phone (F-T4); move-then-one-action framing in UI copy.

## 4. Staged redesign plan

Principle: preserve the deterministic core and its invariants untouched; change *supply* (world/facts), *choice* (scheduler scoring), and *table ritual* (shell) in independently shippable, independently falsifiable stages. No stage depends on AI/prompt changes.

### Phase 0 — Hygiene + measurement + truth-boundary honesty (days; risk: minimal)
Doc fixes (F-D1), `verifyClosing` boundary fix (F-P3), solo-brief symmetry (F-F3), **closing path-independence (F-P4)** — a contained change to `buildClosingPrompt` inputs plus a public-only field computation — delete-or-wire the dead `NON_CARD_NAME_WHITELIST`, eval telemetry extension (Q10.1) + frozen baseline.
*Player impact:* the closing stops citing evidence the table may never have seen — a direct trust fix. *Acceptance:* tests green (including F-P4's regression tests, Q11); baseline JSON checked in under `docs/research/`.

### Phase 1 — Scheduler choice layer (≈1 session; risk: low)
Best-of-K selection with extended experiential score; hard note-distinctness rule; fact-kind caps as scoring terms first (hard only if eval proves feasibility ≥99%); **public-only convergence floor** (Q8a) as a third checkpoint, feasibility-swept on 500 seeds before enforcement (current public-only means suggest it is nearly free). Thread-staging terms activate in Phase 3 but the scoring scaffold lands here.
*Dependencies:* Phase 0 telemetry. *Player impact:* immediate — more varied mechanism mix and non-twin notes, every case still provably fair. *Acceptance:* on 500 seeds — accounting-fact share <45%, note room/item share <50%, no same-kind run ≥3, success ≥99.8%, ms/mystery <10× baseline. *Risk:* score overfitting to metrics — mitigated by playtest questionnaire (Q10.4).

### Phase 2 — Turn-shell fidelity + evidence capsule (≈1–2 sessions; risk: low, UI-scoped)
F-T1 butler ritual + pantry reminder + Evidence-Room rule; F-T2 completion — "item cards" wording, phone-side penalty instruction, payment acknowledgement + log entry, elimination handling; F-T3 passage de-randomization (variant-gated); F-T4 note parity; F-T5 wire `roomClosure`→`lockedRooms`; F-T7 structured suggestion log; seeded shell RNG; **F-P5 evidence capsule** — a deterministic per-reveal write-down summary displayed alongside Ashe's narration on host and phone (DVD-authentic; makes the player-facing truth channel deterministic end-to-end).
*Dependencies:* none on engine. *Player impact:* high — restores the physical card economy's tension and rulebook rhythm. *Acceptance:* state-machine tests (Q11); a scripted playthrough exercises summon→pantry, wrong accusation→payment→elimination, locked door→unlock; blind-playtest "cheap accusation" score improves. *Risk:* ritual friction — keep every instruction one screen, one acknowledgement.

### Phase 3 — World & fact supply (≈2–3 sessions; risk: medium)
Occasion-conditioned catalogs (Q4); catalog expansion (F-W2); item lifecycle reskins + `item_moved`/`key_event` kinds (Q5, F-F1); answer-room exclusion relaxed to answer-time-only (F-W4); thread stage-facts (F-F2 fix, harvest side).
*Dependencies:* Phase 0 tests; Phase 1 scoring (so new supply is actually *chosen*). *Player impact:* high on replay — cases become topologically distinct by occasion; notes and clues draw from join/key semantics. *Acceptance:* golden-corpus #3/#10 tests pass; per-family invariant sweeps 100%; occasion divergence measurable (Q10.5); accounting share <35%. *Risk:* the biggest of the plan — new kinds must be exhaustively property-tested; sequence one kind at a time behind the eval harness.

### Phase 4 — Arc & convergence shaping (≈1–2 sessions; risk: low-medium)
Thread setup/payoff scheduling rules (Q6); converged-pair steering (Q7.3); N1/N2 job differentiation using the new kinds (Q8); consider times-min scoring pressure.
*Dependencies:* Phases 1+3. *Player impact:* medium-high — round-to-round tension (unresolved threads), varied endgames, distinct note experiences. *Acceptance:* ≥80% of cases stage a complete thread arc; converged-pair distribution within ±10% of target weights; note-kind identity <30% room/item; meta-telegraph probe (Q10.3) drops to near-chance.

### Phase 5 — Event economy (≈2–3 sessions; risk: medium, shell-scoped)
Post-turn event queue keyed to clue positions (replacing wall-clock interruptions as default): facedown pantry-card placements at board locations, Inspector's Challenge, door lock/unlock events sourced from world state, option unlocks. Events manipulate *cards and access*, never assert world facts — so engine truthfulness is untouched by construction.
*Dependencies:* Phase 2 (rituals, locked-door plumbing). *Player impact:* highest on round rhythm — the brief's closing hypothesis, which I endorse (F-T6). *Acceptance:* every event is post-turn (rulebook), truth-neutral by code review + test, and playtests report rounds "changed state" between reveals.

Deliberately excluded: engine/scheduler replacement, answer-aware prose, auto-marking eliminations, public candidate counts, adjacency simulation, letting public clues converge all four axes.

## 6. Top five conclusions

1. **The deterministic core is sound and verified — the genericity is a supply-and-choice problem layered on top of it.** All fair-play invariants hold in code, tests, and my 500-seed reproduction; the fix path is catalogs, fact kinds, and schedule *selection*, never architecture replacement.
2. **The cheapest high-leverage fix is best-of-K schedule selection** with an experiential score (kind diversity, note distinctness, thread staging, converged-pair novelty): attempts cost ~26 ms, every candidate is already proven fair, and today the engine returns the first feasible schedule with its quality score computed and discarded (`clue-scheduler.ts:428`).
3. **The case shape is a monoculture:** item+time converge in 95.4% of cases, time collapses to one candidate in 57.8% (devaluing dealt time cards), items are bimodally 2–3-or-5, 52.5% of reveals and 69.9% of notes are accounting facts, and 40% of cases schedule zero innocent-thread facts. Each case is fair; the *meta-game* telegraphs.
4. **The turn shell's biggest authentic-rules gaps are economic, not informational:** the 0–4 accusation count is authentic (earlier leak framing rejected), and the host screen already suggests turning in cards — but the penalty is the wrong card type, invisible on the accuser's phone, unrecorded, and never enforces elimination; the Summon-the-Butler pantry draw that feeds the item economy was dropped; an unseeded secret-passage minigame was invented; and the app never wired its own simulated room closures into its own locked-room UI.
5. **Two honesty gaps sit at the prose boundary, both cheaply closable:** the pre-generated closing narrates both optional private notes as evidence "revealed during play" and a with-notes final field, though the ten public facts alone leave a far broader field (7.83/4.16/7.75/3.72 mean marginals — reproduced); and clue verification checks card names only, never scope/polarity/entailment (the claimed invented-people audit is dead code). Fixes: a path-independent closing, and the DVD-authentic deterministic evidence capsule displayed with Ashe's narration — making the truth channel deterministic end-to-end. Round-to-round dynamism should then come from a truth-neutral post-turn event economy plus staged innocent-thread arcs — events that move cards and access, never facts.
